Bathroom Remodel Cost in 2026

By Renue Home Editorial Team · Updated September 30, 2026

Your bathroom budget starts with the work you want done. Replacing a vanity, rebuilding a shower and replacing every fixture are different projects. A single national average can hide those differences.

For a starting reference, Angi reports an overall bathroom remodel average of $12,151 in its July 9, 2026 guide. Its separate project-scope estimates run from $3,000 to $10,000 for minor work, $10,000 to $25,000 for a partial remodel and $25,000 to $80,000 for a full remodel. Those figures describe different scopes, so the overall average is not a full-remodel quote. Source and scope definitions from Angi.

Price the shower separately when that is the whole job

A shower project should have its own scope. Angi's July 15, 2026 walk-in shower guide reports a $9,000 average and a $6,000 to $12,000 range. That shower estimate is not a price for renovating the rest of the bathroom. Our walk-in shower cost guide compares published references and shows what to ask about demolition, drainage, waterproofing and glass.

If you want both a shower and a full room refresh, ask the contractor to show the shower work and remaining room work separately. Check whether the room total already includes the shower. Adding a shower estimate to an all-inclusive bathroom quote would count the same work twice.

Turn your ideas into a written scope

Before a site visit, make a simple keep-or-replace list. Include the tub, shower, toilet, vanity, floor, lighting and ventilation. Mark any fixture you want moved. Then describe the result you need, such as easier entry, more storage or repair of damaged surfaces.

  • Product selections. Ask for product names or model numbers. Where you have not selected a product, ask what dollar allowance is included.
  • Preparation. Ask what demolition, disposal, wall or floor preparation and plumbing work are included.
  • Completion. Confirm who handles patching, painting, final cleanup and any required inspections.
  • Unknown conditions. Ask how hidden damage will be documented and priced before additional work proceeds.
  • Schedule. Ask when materials will be available and how long the bathroom will be unusable. A work start date and a usable-bathroom date are different commitments.

Compare complete costs, not just headline prices

Use the same scope for each bidder. A lower total may include a different shower system, smaller material allowances or fewer finishing tasks. The useful comparison is what you will receive for the total amount you pay.

Start with each written base quote. Add required work that the quote excludes and the difference between its allowances and your selected products. Keep your reserve for unknown work separate, since it is not an agreed contractor charge. If financing is offered, compare its total repayment with the cash price and review the financing terms before signing.

When a smaller project may be enough

If your concern is appearance, request an option that keeps the layout and working fixtures. If the concern is a leak or damaged material, ask the professional to explain the underlying repair before choosing finishes. A cosmetic update and a repair should not be treated as interchangeable options.

If resale is your main goal, discuss the proposed scope with a local real estate professional. A national cost-recovery percentage cannot tell you what a specific buyer will pay for your home. Compare the likely local benefit with the project cost and your own use of the room.

Questions about bathroom remodel costs

Can I price the job by square foot?

Use square footage to describe the room, then ask for a scope-based quote. A toilet, vanity and shower still need individual pricing. Two rooms with the same floor area can require different plumbing, products and preparation.

Does a quoted remodel include permits?

Ask the contractor to identify required permits, who obtains them and whether the fees are included. Confirm local requirements with the building department. Do not assume that every quote includes the same permit costs.

How can I reduce the budget without losing track of the scope?

Ask for separately priced alternatives, such as keeping the existing layout or choosing a different vanity. Have the contractor show exactly which work and products change with each option.
